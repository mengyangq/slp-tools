      About this map

      A companion to a scoping review of artificial intelligence in aphasia research.

      This map shows 412 peer-reviewed studies, published between January 1, 2010 and June 30, 2026, in which a method that learns from data (machine learning, deep learning, natural language processing, automatic speech recognition, or generative AI) was applied to people with aphasia or to aphasic language. The studies were identified by a search of seven databases and archives run on July 27, 2026, screened and charted from their abstracts, and organized by the similarity of their abstracts into twelve research communities, each named from its own vocabulary.

      How to read it

      Each point is one study. Studies whose abstracts are similar sit close together and are joined by a line; colour shows the community a study belongs to. Larger points have more connections. Hubs (ringed) are the most connected studies in their community; bridges (diamonds) connect communities. Click a point to open the study, its neighbours, and a link to the publisher's page. Use the filters on the left to narrow the map by year, community, method, population, or what each abstract reports about how the work was evaluated.

      What each study shows

      Each study card gives the title, authors, year, venue, and DOI, taken from the publisher's record in Crossref; a short list of keywords drawn from the abstract; and the characteristics charted for the review, which describe what the abstract reports. The one-sentence summary, where shown, is Semantic Scholar's automatically generated TLDR. Abstracts are not reproduced here.

      Snapshot

      The map is a snapshot of the literature as of the search date, July 27, 2026. It is not updated as new studies appear. Positions and community boundaries are properties of this map; a study near a boundary could reasonably belong to either neighbour.

      Glossary

      Aphasia
      An acquired language disorder, most often after stroke; primary progressive aphasia (PPA) is a form that begins gradually with neurodegenerative disease.
      Community
      A group of studies whose abstracts are more similar to one another than to the rest of the map. Communities were found by a standard network method and named by reading the vocabulary that sets each group apart.
      Hub
      One of the three studies with the most connections inside its community.
      Bridge
      One of the ten studies that most often lie on the shortest paths between communities; they connect lines of research.
      Classical machine learning
      Methods such as support vector machines, random forests, and regression models that learn from hand-chosen features.
      Deep learning
      Neural networks with many layers that learn their own features from raw data such as speech or images.
      Generative AI
      Models, including large language models, that produce text, speech, or images rather than only classifying them.
      Tested on independent data
      The abstract reports evaluation on data that were not used to build the model, such as a held-out dataset or a different site.
      Evaluated with people with aphasia
      The abstract reports that people with aphasia used or were assessed with the tool, whether as a feasibility or usability study or as a clinical evaluation.
