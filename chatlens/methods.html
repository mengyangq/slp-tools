Privacy

ChatLens runs entirely in your browser. Transcripts are parsed and analyzed on your computer and are never uploaded to any server. After the first visit the page works offline, and nothing is downloaded at any point.

What ChatLens reads

CHAT (.cha) files, or plain .txt in CLAN format: @headers, main tiers (*CHI:), and dependent tiers (%mor, %gra, %pho, and others). Gem marks (@Bg/@Eg) and time-alignment bullets are read too, powering the Sections and Rate tools.

Both %mor styles are supported, and ChatLens works out which one it is looking at:

classic MOR/POST   det:art|the n|doggie-PL v|fall&PAST adv|down .
UD (Batchalign2)   det|the-Def-Art noun|dog-Plur aux|be-Fin-Pres verb|run-Part .

They differ in a way that matters for counting. Classic MOR segments: the -PL in n|doggie-PL is a morpheme you can point at. UD instead describes a word with feature values (-Plur, -Pres, -Nom); those are labels on the whole word, not pieces of it. TalkBank put it plainly: “its control of morphological analysis on the %mor line is not as analytic as MOR” — which is why they still maintain MOR grammars for English, French, Spanish and Mandarin. So ChatLens uses a UD tier for what it is good at — part of speech and lemmas — and reports no morpheme count at all from one.

ChatLens does not annotate

It reads annotation; it does not produce it. There is no rules engine, no part-of-speech tagger and no neural model in this tool, and there is no way to turn one on. That is deliberate. Producing %mor is CLAN's job, and it does it with decades of per-language lexicon work behind it. A morpheme count estimated here that disagreed with CLAN's would not teach you that two estimators differ — it would teach you that one of the two tools is broken, without telling you which. So when a measure needs annotation this file does not have, ChatLens says so and shows you how to get it, rather than guessing.

The same rule governs phonology: consonant clusters and phoneme counts are read from a %pho tier or not at all. English spelling is a poor guide to pronunciation (through, thought, tough), so a count derived from letters would contradict any real transcription.

Reading the badges

Every number tells you where it came from, and there are only three answers. exact · %mor means it was computed from the file's classic MOR %mor tier and is exact given that tagging. main tier marks measures counted directly from the words spoken; these need no morphology and work for any language. needs %mor means the file does not carry the annotation this measure requires — ChatLens will not fill the gap with a guess.

Which words count

Counted words exclude: unintelligible material (xxx, yyy, www), repeated or revised material ([/], [//], [///], [/-], [/?]; the material before the marker) and material marked excluded with [e], fillers in either notation (&-um or the older bare &um), events (&=laughs), sound fragments (&+…), omitted words (0is), pauses, and annotation codes. Following CLAN's MLU defaults (manual p100), the bare hesitations uh and um and any form beginning 0 & + - $ are also left out of MLU, though they still count as word tokens for frequency and TTR, as they do in CLAN's FREQ. An utterance postcoded [+ mlue] is dropped from MLU entirely; other postcodes such as [+ exc] and [+ bch] require an explicit switch in CLAN, so they are counted here and reported under “How is this calculated?” instead. A line opening with +, completes the same speaker's previous broken-off line, and the pair counts as one utterance. Replacements like goggie [: doggie] are analyzed as the intended word. Shortened forms like (be)cause are completed. Compounds (peanut_butter) are one word and one morpheme. Contractions (it's, don't) are one word and two morphemes. Word length is counted in characters of the word as written, apostrophe included (doggie's = 8), matching CLAN's WDLEN. The transcript panel shows every one of these decisions: excluded material is faded or struck through, and hovering explains why.

Sample window (skip / read a maximum)

Clinical work often uses a fixed-size sample: skip the warm-up, then analyze the next 50 or 100 utterances. The Sample row above the tabs does this: skip first N utterances and read a maximum of M utterances, applied when you press Apply filters.

Both numbers are counted per speaker, in speaking order, the same convention as CLAN's +z switch: skip 10 with a maximum of 50 analyzes each speaker's utterances 11–60 (in CLAN: mlu +t*CHI +z11u-60u file.cha). Counting is over raw utterances as they appear in the file, before any MLU exclusions, so an utterance that is later excluded from MLU (say, one containing xxx) still uses up one slot of the window. That matches how CLAN reads ranges and keeps the window a property of the transcript, not of any one measure.

While a window is applied, everything follows it: every tab, the transcript panel (which shows scissors marks where the selected speaker's material was skipped or cut off), the Search, Sections, Rate, and Sounds tools, the summary CSV, and the printable report (both note the window). Two things always read the whole file: Check (it validates the file itself) and the tagged-.cha download (so your corrected file is complete). Clearing the filters restores the full transcript.

Numbering. The transcript panel and Search number utterances: 1 is the first utterance in the file, headers and dependent tiers are not counted, and the numbering does not change when a window is applied (a window that skips material simply starts at a higher number, marked with scissors). Hovering a number shows the raw file line as well; file lines are what the Check tool reports, for fixing the file in CLAN or a text editor.

MLU, MLU5, and MLT

An utterance counts toward MLU when it has at least one countable word. By default, utterances containing unintelligible speech are left out entirely (the stricter, SALT-style convention); a checkbox above the tabs switches to counting their intelligible words instead (the CLAN-style convention).

MLU = total words (or morphemes) ÷ counted utterances

MLU5 = mean length of the 5 longest utterances

MLT = utterances ÷ turns, and words ÷ turns (a turn = a run of back-to-back utterances by the same speaker)

The "How is this calculated?" panel on each tab shows these formulas with your file's actual numbers plugged in, including the exact denominator used.

Two caveats the panel will flag for you when they apply. If a speaker has fewer than five counted utterances, MLU5 is the mean of however many there are, and is not comparable with MLU5 from a longer sample. And if some counted utterances have no morpheme analysis at all — a file in a language with no %mor tier and no morpheme rules — MLU (morphemes) is averaged over only the utterances that do have one, so it rests on a smaller set than MLU (words).

Counting morphemes (Brown, 1973)

Inflections count: plural -s, possessive 's, third-person -s, regular past -ed, progressive -ing, and contracted parts (n't, 're, 'll). Irregular pasts and plurals (went, feet) count as one morpheme. Compounds, proper names, and playful reduplications count as one. Catenatives (gonna, wanna) count as two. Plural possessives count all three parts: dogs' = dog + -s + 's. In classic MOR terms: each base word, each -suffix, and each prefix# adds one; fused features (&PAST, &3S) add nothing; clitics (~) count separately. A compound keeps an inflection sitting on it (n|+n|birth+n|day-PL = 2 morphemes). On a UD tier every field after the lemma is a feature and adds nothing — see "What ChatLens reads" above.

When a file has a %mor tier but some lines do not line up with the words spoken, those lines are left out of the morpheme mean rather than guessed at; the MLU tab says how many, and Advanced › Check lists them so you can fix them in CLAN. A %mor tier may either include entries for retraced material or leave them out — both layouts line up correctly.

Lexical diversity

TTR = types ÷ tokens over the speaker's counted words (lower-cased). Because TTR falls as samples grow, ChatLens also reports MATTR (average TTR over a sliding 50-word window) and VOCD-D (random samples of 35 to 50 words, 100 draws, 3 runs, fixed seed, curve fit following Malvern and Richards). Numbers are comparable within one tool, not across tools: CLAN, SALT, and others apply slightly different conventions. ChatLens documents its rules here and labels every number's source.

Which words go into "tokens". Every intelligible word the speaker produced, including words in utterances that MLU leaves out. An utterance containing xxx is dropped from MLU as a whole, but the words in it were still said, so they count towards the vocabulary measures and the frequency lists. That is why the Overview can show, say, 1 utterance analyzed and 10 word tokens.

When VOCD cannot answer. If nearly every word in the sample is different, the curve fit has nothing to converge on and D is effectively unbounded. ChatLens says > 400 rather than printing the top of its search range as though it were a measurement. It usually means the sample is too short for VOCD; use MATTR, or collect more words.

Adding a %mor tier (recommended for exact morphology)

Option A: CLAN MOR (English, French, Spanish, Mandarin; finest detail):

1. Install CLAN and the MOR grammar for your language
   (talkbank.org > Programs > CLAN; "morgrams" for grammars)
2. In CLAN's Commands window, set lib to the grammar folder, then:
     check +epsfile.cha        (validate the CHAT file first)
     mor  file.cha             (recent CLAN also runs POST + MEGRASP;
     post file.cha              older versions: run each separately)
     megrasp file.cha
3. Fix any words MOR flags as unknown, re-run, and load the
   tagged file into ChatLens.

Option B: Batchalign2 (UD/Stanza; about 27 languages):

uv tool install batchalign
batchalign morphotag --lang=eng  input_dir  output_dir

Both write ordinary %mor/%gra tiers into the CHAT file. ChatLens detects which system produced them.

Advanced tools

Search (like CLAN's KWAL) lists every utterance containing a word, with line numbers. Sections (like GEM) analyzes activities marked with @Bg/@Eg separately, so contexts can be compared. Rate (like TIMEDUR) uses time-alignment bullets to report speaking time, words per minute, and utterances per minute; line-level times make these approximate. Sounds (like PHONFREQ) tallies IPA consonants and vowels from the %pho tier and counts consonant clusters at word edges (spelling-based estimates when there is no %pho). Check is a light version of CLAN's CHECK: file structure, parser notes, and %mor/%gra alignment against the spoken words.

Known limitations

MLU in morphemes is English-shaped, in CLAN and therefore here

Morpheme counts come from CLAN's own countMorphs(), and that function traces a fixed list of English morphemes: plural, past, possessive, plural-possessive, present participle, contracted negative, contracted auxiliary. The CLAN manual states the list and adds “We are working on creating a list of morphemes to track for other languages.” Some of the markers it tests are cross-linguistic UD feature names (-Plur, -Past), so a Spanish or German file does get a number — but nothing in the list covers case, gender agreement, aspect particles or classifiers. Treat MLU in morphemes for a non-English sample as a partial count, not a complete morphological analysis. MLU in words, MLT and lexical diversity carry no such assumption.

Why we do not offer morpheme counting for Spanish or French

We checked the acquisition literature before deciding. For both languages the field has moved away from morpheme-based MLU deliberately, and there is no convention a tool could implement without inventing one.

Spanish. Gutiérrez-Clellen, Restrepo, Bedore, Peña & Anderson (2000, LSHSS 31, 88–98) compared four methods of computing Spanish MLU in morphemes and found significant inconsistencies between them. The published conventions disagree by a factor of two to four on a single word: SALT's Spanish rules mark only the plural -s, explicitly declining to decompose verb conjugations or count diminutives, so corrieron is one morpheme; the Linares (1981) and Blume & Lust criteria decompose it into root, theme vowel and inflection. Aguado (1988, Infancia y Aprendizaje 43, 75–95) found the two MLU types correlate at r = .99 in Spanish, so little is lost. Baron, Bedore, Peña, Lovgren-Uribe, López & Villagrana (2018, AJSLP 27, 975–987) state the field position: MLUw "has become widely accepted for languages other than English", given "a lack of an adequate developmental frame of reference in morpheme-rich languages". What discriminates in Spanish is accuracy on articles, direct-object clitics, verb inflection and subjunctive in obligatory contexts — Castilla-Earls, Pérez-Leroux, Fulcher-Rood & Barr (2021, LSHSS 52, 497–511) report 85% sensitivity and 91% specificity from verb and subjunctive accuracy alone; Bedore & Leonard (2005, Applied Psycholinguistics 26, 195–225) found Spanish-speaking children with SLI accurate on verb inflections but impaired on articles and clitics.

French. The same conclusion, for partly different reasons: word and morpheme MLU correlate at r = .991, French carries relatively few overt markers, and counting words avoids depending on "decisions made by the researcher and/or categories of automatic analyzer used for morphemic segmentation". There is also a complication peculiar to French — much of its verb inflection is silent. je parle, tu parles and ils parlent are homophones, so a morpheme count read off spelling would credit distinctions the child did not audibly produce.

So ChatLens reports exact word-level measures for these languages and says so on the MLU tab, rather than picking one convention and producing a number with no normative comparison behind it. Obligatory- context accuracy is the measure worth adding next; it needs error coding rather than counting, so it is not in this version.

As far as we can tell no per-language morpheme list has been published for CLAN — the manual's “we are working on” still stands, and KIDEVAL's other-language support (+lfra, +lspa, +ljpn, +lzho and so on) supplies comparison norms rather than a different morpheme counter. If you know otherwise, we would like to hear.

Morpheme-level measures require a classic MOR %mor tier — there is no estimator, by design (see “ChatLens does not annotate” above). The contractible/uncontractible copula and auxiliary distinction is approximated as contracted versus not contracted. No dependency-based syntax measures yet (DSS, IPSyn), though the %gra tier is read. Brown's 14 are English-only by construction and are not offered for other languages. VOCD may differ slightly from CLAN's implementation, which runs on lemmas from %mor by default. Speaking rates from line-level times include within-line pauses.

Brown's counts are occurrence tallies, not percentages supplied in obligatory contexts, so they do not by themselves establish mastery. Postcodes other than [+ mlue] are read and reported but do not exclude anything, matching CLAN's defaults — the MLU tab shows what excluding each would do. These are inherent to the measures rather than gaps in the implementation.
