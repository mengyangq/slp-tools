Privacy

ChatLens runs entirely in your browser. Transcripts are parsed and analyzed on your computer and are never uploaded to any server. After the first visit the page also works offline. The optional neural tagger downloads a model to your browser; your data still never leaves your computer.

What ChatLens reads

CHAT (.cha) files, or plain .txt in CLAN format: @headers, main tiers (*CHI:), and dependent tiers (%mor, %gra, %pho, and others). Both %mor styles are supported: classic MOR/POST notation (n|doggie-PL) and UD-style output from Batchalign2/Stanza. TalkBank is moving to UD; classic MOR remains available for English, French, Spanish, and Mandarin, and gives the finest morphological detail. Gem marks (@Bg/@Eg) and time-alignment bullets are read too, powering the Sections and Rate tools.

Reading the badges

Every number tells you where it came from. exact · %mor means it was computed from the file's %mor tier and is exact given that tagging. estimated · rules means ChatLens applied its deterministic English word-ending rules (Brown-style) because the file has no %mor tier. estimated · tagger means a part-of-speech tagger running in your browser made the call. main tier marks measures counted directly from the words spoken; these need no morphology and work for any language.

Which words count

Counted words exclude: unintelligible material (xxx, yyy, www), repeated or revised material ([/], [//], [///]; the material before the marker), fillers (&-um), events (&=laughs), sound fragments (&+…), omitted words (0is), pauses, and annotation codes. Replacements like goggie [: doggie] are analyzed as the intended word. Shortened forms like (be)cause are completed. Compounds (peanut_butter) are one word and one morpheme. Contractions (it's, don't) are one word and two morphemes. Word length is counted in characters of the word as written, apostrophe included (doggie's = 8), matching CLAN's WDLEN. The transcript panel shows every one of these decisions: excluded material is faded or struck through, and hovering explains why.

Sample window (skip / read a maximum)

Clinical work often uses a fixed-size sample: skip the warm-up, then analyze the next 50 or 100 utterances. The Sample row above the tabs does this: skip first N utterances and read a maximum of M utterances, applied when you press Apply filters.

Both numbers are counted per speaker, in speaking order, the same convention as CLAN's +z switch: skip 10 with a maximum of 50 analyzes each speaker's utterances 11–60 (in CLAN: mlu +t*CHI +z11u-60u file.cha). Counting is over raw utterances as they appear in the file, before any MLU exclusions, so an utterance that is later excluded from MLU (say, one containing xxx) still uses up one slot of the window. That matches how CLAN reads ranges and keeps the window a property of the transcript, not of any one measure.

While a window is applied, everything follows it: every tab, the transcript panel (which shows scissors marks where the selected speaker's material was skipped or cut off), the Search, Sections, Rate, and Sounds tools, the summary CSV, and the printable report (both note the window). Two things always read the whole file: Check (it validates the file itself) and the tagged-.cha download (so your corrected file is complete). Clearing the filters restores the full transcript.

Numbering. The transcript panel and Search number utterances: 1 is the first utterance in the file, headers and dependent tiers are not counted, and the numbering does not change when a window is applied (a window that skips material simply starts at a higher number, marked with scissors). Hovering a number shows the raw file line as well; file lines are what the Check tool reports, for fixing the file in CLAN or a text editor.

MLU, MLU5, and MLT

An utterance counts toward MLU when it has at least one countable word. By default, utterances containing unintelligible speech are left out entirely (the stricter, SALT-style convention); a checkbox above the tabs switches to counting their intelligible words instead (the CLAN-style convention).

MLU = total words (or morphemes) ÷ counted utterances

MLU5 = mean length of the 5 longest utterances

MLT = utterances ÷ turns, and words ÷ turns (a turn = a run of back-to-back utterances by the same speaker)

The "How is this calculated?" panel on each tab shows these formulas with your file's actual numbers plugged in.

Counting morphemes (Brown, 1973)

Inflections count: plural -s, possessive 's, third-person -s, regular past -ed, progressive -ing, and contracted parts (n't, 're, 'll). Irregular pasts and plurals (went, feet) count as one morpheme. Compounds, proper names, and playful reduplications count as one. Catenatives (gonna, wanna) count as two. In %mor terms: each base word, each -suffix, and each prefix# adds one; fused features (&PAST, &3S) add nothing; clitics (~) count separately.

Lexical diversity

TTR = types ÷ tokens over the speaker's counted words (lower-cased). Because TTR falls as samples grow, ChatLens also reports MATTR (average TTR over a sliding 50-word window) and VOCD-D (random samples of 35 to 50 words, 100 draws, 3 runs, fixed seed, curve fit following Malvern and Richards). Numbers are comparable within one tool, not across tools: CLAN, SALT, and others apply slightly different conventions. ChatLens documents its rules here and labels every number's source.

Adding a %mor tier (recommended for exact morphology)

Option A: CLAN MOR (English, French, Spanish, Mandarin; finest detail):

1. Install CLAN and the MOR grammar for your language
   (talkbank.org > Programs > CLAN; "morgrams" for grammars)
2. In CLAN's Commands window, set lib to the grammar folder, then:
     check +epsfile.cha        (validate the CHAT file first)
     mor  file.cha             (recent CLAN also runs POST + MEGRASP;
     post file.cha              older versions: run each separately)
     megrasp file.cha
3. Fix any words MOR flags as unknown, re-run, and load the
   tagged file into ChatLens.

Option B: Batchalign2 (UD/Stanza; about 27 languages):

uv tool install batchalign
batchalign morphotag --lang=eng  input_dir  output_dir

Both write ordinary %mor/%gra tiers into the CHAT file. ChatLens detects which system produced them.

Advanced tools

Search (like CLAN's KWAL) lists every utterance containing a word, with line numbers. Sections (like GEM) analyzes activities marked with @Bg/@Eg separately, so contexts can be compared. Rate (like TIMEDUR) uses time-alignment bullets to report speaking time, words per minute, and utterances per minute; line-level times make these approximate. Sounds (like PHONFREQ) tallies IPA consonants and vowels from the %pho tier and counts consonant clusters at word edges (spelling-based estimates when there is no %pho). Check is a light version of CLAN's CHECK: file structure, parser notes, and %mor/%gra alignment against the spoken words.

In-browser tagger (beta)

The neural tagger loads transformers.js from a CDN and a part-of-speech model from the Hugging Face Hub, then tags locally in your browser. It needs internet for the one-time download and works offline afterwards. Built-in choices: English (BERT, UD part-of-speech tags), French (CamemBERT, French Treebank tags), and Chinese (BERT); you can also paste any compatible token-classification model id. No converted Spanish model exists on the Hub yet; for Spanish, use the %mor route above. Accuracy on child speech has not been validated; treat results as exploratory and prefer %mor for reportable numbers.

Known limitations

Morpheme estimation on plain transcripts is English-only for now (Spanish and French morphology needs a %mor tier, pending validated counting conventions). The contractible/uncontractible copula and auxiliary distinction is approximated as contracted versus not contracted. No dependency-based syntax measures yet (DSS, IPSyn). VOCD may differ slightly from CLAN's implementation. Spelling-based cluster detection is approximate; use %pho for exact clusters. Speaking rates from line-level times include within-line pauses.
